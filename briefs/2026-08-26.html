Tier 1Agentic AI and MCPHigh impactGap window, August 20 to 22

The agent protocol layer consolidated under one foundation and then published its next roadmap: agent identity and progressive tool discovery are the priorities

Two dated events, three days apart, that belong together. On August 20 the Agentic AI Foundation, a Linux Foundation body, announced that Google's Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol had formally joined it, placing A2A alongside Anthropic's Model Context Protocol under one neutral governance umbrella. The two operate at different layers: MCP handles the vertical link between an agent and its tools, A2A governs how agents talk to each other. AAIF says it has grown from under 40 members at its December 2025 launch to more than 250, including Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Anthropic, OpenAI, Bloomberg, Shopify and Block. Source: developers.googleblog.com, axios.com

Then on August 22 the MCP core maintainers published an updated roadmap with five priority areas: agentic messaging primitives, HTTP-native transport unification, agent identity and enterprise-ready security, improved primitives, and SDK developer experience. Two of these are the ones to watch. First, agent identity: MCP authorization today assumes a person approving access in a browser, and the roadmap explicitly targets cloud-workload agents with their own identity acting for an absent user, via DPoP, Workload Identity Federation, the ID-JAG grant and standard token exchange. Second, progressive discovery: the maintainers state plainly that connecting to a server with a hundred tools makes the model pay for that whole surface before the user asks anything, and that tool selection degrades as the list grows. The roadmap follows the 2026-07-28 release, which removed protocol-level sessions and the initialization handshake so servers can scale horizontally without holding state. Source: blog.modelcontextprotocol.io, 2026-07-28 spec release

Why it matters: The interesting part is not the governance photo opportunity, it is what the roadmap admits. "Connecting to a server with a hundred tools means the model pays for that entire surface" is a vendor conceding that the current design does not survive contact with a real enterprise tool estate, and progressive discovery is the fix. If you are building on MCP today, tool-count bloat is now a known protocol-level problem with a named workstream, not something you have to solve alone with clever server splitting. Two falsifiable tests over the next two quarters. First, whether agent identity ships as specification rather than extension: the roadmap routes it through IETF OAuth and WIMSE work, which is slow, so an extension-only landing would signal the enterprise identity story slipped again. Second, whether A2A and MCP under one roof actually converge or merely coexist: watch whether any SEP proposes shared identity or transport primitives across both, because shared governance without shared primitives is just a logo on a slide.

Nvidia is committing about $6 billion to Poolside, moving upstream from selling training hardware into building open-weight models itself

The Wall Street Journal reported on August 24 that Nvidia is committing roughly $6 billion to a combined technology and investment agreement with the AI startup Poolside, framed as an attempt to strengthen the US position in open-weight models. The reported structure is unusual: about $1 billion of direct investment, access to Poolside's technology, and the transfer of more than 100 engineers into Nvidia's Nemotron model effort, with Poolside's founders staying at the startup rather than joining Nvidia. Source: wsj.com, techstartups.com

The competitive context is concrete. Chinese labs currently hold the open-weight frontier: Moonshot's Kimi K3 published weights on July 27 with 2.8 trillion total parameters and 104 billion active, DeepSeek V4 Pro graduated from preview on August 12 and reports 80.6 percent on SWE-bench Verified under an MIT licence, and Qwen and GLM ship on a fast cadence. Source: llm-stats.com, llmgateway.io. This is also not an isolated Nvidia move: the same week it was reported in talks to invest in Perplexity at a valuation above $30 billion, and on August 17 it was reported backing up to $105 billion of financing for an OpenAI data centre in Ohio. Source: cnbc.com

Why it matters: A supplier that funds its own customers' competitors is buying demand, not returns, and this deal takes that one layer further: Nvidia is now staffing a model team rather than only financing one. The acqui-licence structure is the detail worth copying into your mental model of the next year, because it moves intellectual property and a hundred engineers without a takeover, and therefore without the antitrust review a takeover would attract. Two falsifiable tests. First, whether a Nemotron open-weight release carrying Poolside lineage appears before the end of Q1 2027 and clears DeepSeek V4 Pro's 80.6 percent on SWE-bench Verified: below that, the $6 billion bought talent rather than a frontier. Second, whether the licence is genuinely open weight with commercial use permitted, or a restricted community licence, because "US alternative to Chinese open models" only holds if developers can actually use it the way they use an MIT-licensed DeepSeek.
Tier 1Anthropic and ClaudeMedium impactIn window, August 25

Anthropic merged Claude's chat and Cowork memory into one store, and published the list of categories it refuses to remember

On August 25 Anthropic unified the memory systems behind Claude chat and Claude Cowork, so context learned in one surface is available in the other. When Cowork runs a task in the cloud it now carries what Claude learned in chat, and the reverse. Memories are organised by topic under Settings, Memory, and can be read, edited or deleted individually, and entries update as a conversation proceeds rather than being written as an end-of-conversation summary. Memory is on by default for Free, Pro and Max, and off by default for Team and Enterprise organisations. Source: techcrunch.com, engadget.com

The privacy design is the part with a hard edge. Sensitive categories including health data, race, ethnicity, religious belief, political view and gender identity are excluded by default, behind an opt-in named Include sensitive topics in memory, and the app notifies the user each time a sensitive topic is saved. A second, narrower set is never stored at all regardless of the setting: government-issued identification, Social Security numbers, criminal history and immigration status. Source: siliconangle.com

Why it matters: A published, categorical never-store list is a more testable commitment than a privacy policy paragraph, and it is the first of its kind among the major assistants. It converts a promise into something a security team can audit by attempting to store a passport number and checking whether it appears under Topics. For anyone running Cowork against a real workspace, the practical change is that the cost of context transfer just went to zero across surfaces, which also means the blast radius of a wrong memory grew: a mistaken fact learned in chat now steers unattended cloud tasks. Two falsifiable tests. First, whether Team and Enterprise defaults flip to on within two quarters, which would show the off-by-default posture was a launch precaution rather than a governance position. Second, whether the never-store list survives contact with regulated verticals, where identity documents are the working material: either an exception appears for those workflows, or those workflows stay off Claude memory.

Three moves inside eight days point the same way: $6 billion into Poolside with more than 100 engineers moving into Nemotron on August 24, reported talks on Perplexity above $30 billion the same day, and up to $105 billion of financing behind an OpenAI data centre in Ohio on August 17. The falsifiable version: if Nemotron ships a competitive open-weight coding model in the next two quarters, Nvidia's customers start competing with their supplier's own product, and that changes procurement conversations at every lab that is not OpenAI.

Early signal Agents are being handed transaction authority in regulated sectors before the identity plumbing exists

Scalable Capital connected ChatGPT and Claude to live trading on August 25, three days after the MCP roadmap conceded that its authorization model still assumes a human approving access in a browser. The gap between what is being deployed and what the protocol layer can currently attest is the interesting risk surface. Watch for the first regulator question about who is liable for an agent-initiated trade, and for whether DPoP or Workload Identity Federation adoption accelerates in response.

Early signal Neutral AI infrastructure is going up for sale

Hugging Face exploring a sale above $13 billion is preliminary and may not happen, but the direction is notable: the industry's shared model registry is a strategic asset that several potential buyers would want to own outright. If it sells to a cloud provider or chipmaker, expect a visible migration of competing labs' weights toward alternative hosts within two quarters. That migration, or its absence, is the test of whether neutrality was load-bearing.

Early signal The AI capex bill is reaching consumer prices

Amazon raised Echo, Kindle and Fire TV prices by as much as 60 percent as memory costs climb, attributed to AI infrastructure consuming advanced memory production. This is the first mainstream consumer price move explicitly traced to data centre demand. If it spreads to phones and PCs in the next two quarters, the politics of the buildout change, because the cost stops being an abstraction on a hyperscaler balance sheet.
